Create a cohesive sequence of seven visual tutorial images for a first-person VR assembly game.

GENERAL VISUAL DIRECTION

Show the scene from the player’s first-person VR viewpoint at a compact industrial assembly station. Use clean, readable 3D game artwork matching a stylized industrial workshop. The product being assembled is a small round mechanical device with these parts:

- Cartoon-shell base
- Wind-up spring with a child winding key
- Circular top cover
- Three screws with child turning handles
- Pressure plate
- Hammer
- Physical stamping tool

Show two VR hands/controllers only when relevant. Use the same object proportions, lighting, camera position, and visual language in every image.

Communicate primarily through:

- Translucent ghost poses
- Bright green destination highlights
- Curved motion arrows
- Controller-button highlights
- Small controller diagrams
- Green success glows
- Red “do not do this” accents only where essential

Avoid sentences, paragraphs, floating instruction panels, and excessive labels. If absolutely necessary, only use tiny universal markings such as “×5”, “×3”, a check mark, or an X.

Clearly distinguish the control schemes:

- Grabbing and releasing parts: squeeze and release the controller’s grab/select trigger.
- Winding and tightening: hold the small child handle, then rotate that same hand’s thumbstick in complete clockwise circles. Do not show wrist rotation.
- Seating the pressure plate: physically swing the hammer downward with moderate speed.
- Stamping: physically bring the stamp down onto the completed object.
- Mounted parts remain attached and cannot be pulled back out.

Use green highlights for the currently interactive part and destination. De-emphasize all objects that are not relevant to the current step.

IMAGE 1 — INSERT THE SPRING

Show one VR hand squeezing the grab/select trigger while holding the loose wind-up spring above the open device shell.

Highlight the spring socket inside the shell with a soft green glow. Show a translucent copy of the spring seated in the socket and a short downward placement arrow. Include a small secondary visual showing the controller trigger being released when the spring reaches the socket.

The visual sequence should communicate:

grab spring → place in glowing socket → release trigger

Do not show thumbstick input in this image.

IMAGE 2 — WIND THE SPRING

Show the spring already mounted inside the shell. Highlight only the spring’s small child winding key.

Show one VR hand holding that winding key. Beside the same hand’s controller, show the thumbstick with a bold clockwise circular arrow and a small “×5”.

Show several faint progressive positions around the thumbstick circle and a subtle winding/ratchet effect on the key. The hand and controller should remain mostly stationary; the thumbstick is what circles.

Make it unmistakable that the player circles the thumbstick clockwise five times while continuing to hold the winding key.

Do not depict wrist twisting or physically rotating the whole controller.

IMAGE 3 — PLACE THE COVER

Show the wound spring already installed. One VR hand holds the circular top cover using the grab/select trigger.

Highlight the cover socket on top of the shell in green. Show a translucent correctly aligned cover in its final position, with a short downward arrow. Show the trigger being released once aligned.

The visual sequence should communicate:

grab cover → align over shell → release into socket

Do not show screws yet being manipulated.

IMAGE 4 — INSERT AND TIGHTEN THREE SCREWS

Show the cover mounted and three glowing screw holes arranged around it.

Use a clear two-part visual composition:

Left side:
- A VR hand grabs one loose screw.
- A short arrow moves it into one highlighted screw socket.
- A small trigger-release symbol shows that it should be released into the socket.

Right side:
- The screw is mounted.
- The hand holds the screw’s small child turning handle.
- The same controller’s thumbstick has a clockwise circular arrow marked “×3”.
- Show the screw gradually descending until its head touches the cover.

Also show the other two screw locations with subtle repetition symbols or three green completion dots, communicating that the same process must be completed for all three screws.

Make it clear that the thumbstick circles clockwise three times for each screw. Do not depict wrist rotation or turning the entire controller like a screwdriver.

IMAGE 5 — PLACE THE PRESSURE PLATE

Show all three screws fully seated and the cover secured.

A VR hand holds the loose pressure plate using the grab/select trigger. Highlight the pressure-plate socket at the center/top of the assembly in green. Show a translucent final plate pose and a short placement arrow. Include the trigger-release symbol at the final position.

The visual sequence should communicate:

grab pressure plate → place in center socket → release trigger

Do not show hammering until the next image.

IMAGE 6 — SEAT THE PRESSURE PLATE WITH THE HAMMER

Show the pressure plate mounted but slightly raised. A VR hand physically holds the hammer above it.

Use a strong downward swing arc ending with the hammer head contacting the pressure plate. Communicate a controlled, medium-strength strike visually:

- Green middle portion of the swing arc
- A faint weak strike on one side marked with a small neutral/unfinished symbol
- A dangerous overly forceful strike marked red with a warning burst
- The correct moderate strike ends in a green seated plate and check mark

Do not show a controller button being pressed. This action is controlled by the player’s real physical hammer swing speed.

The image should teach: strike firmly enough to seat the plate, but not so hard that the device detonates.

IMAGE 7 — APPLY THE FINAL STAMP

Show the fully assembled device with its pressure plate seated. A VR hand holds the physical stamping tool directly above the intended stamp surface.

Show a short, decisive downward motion arrow and the stamp contacting only the completed object. At the contact point, show the finished stamp mark appearing with a green confirmation glow and check mark.

Do not show a controller-button prompt. The action is performed through physical contact and downward stamping motion.

The final object should appear complete only after the visible stamp mark is applied.

CONSISTENCY AND CLARITY REQUIREMENTS

- Use one dominant action per image.
- Keep instructional objects large and centered.
- Use consistent green for valid interactions and destinations.
- Use curved arrows only for circular thumbstick input.
- Use straight arrows for placing parts.
- Use swing arcs for hammer and stamp motion.
- Controller highlights must correspond to the hand performing the action.
- Never imply that winding or screw tightening uses wrist rotation.
- Never imply that hammering or stamping uses a controller button.
- Never show parts being removed after socketing.
- Avoid unexplained UI, dialogue boxes, dense text, captions, or decorative clutter.
- Keep every image legible when viewed as a medium-sized in-world tutorial card.
- No extra fingers, malformed controllers, duplicated tools, floating parts, or incorrect assembly order.

For the clearest results, provide screenshots of the actual shell, spring, cover, screws, plate, hammer, stamp, and controller model as visual references.
